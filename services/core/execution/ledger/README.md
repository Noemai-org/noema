# execution/ledger

**What it does.** The Postgres implementation of the controller's `Ledger` and `GrantSource` ports, plus the kill-switch read for `Binder.WritesEnabled`. Every method is one transaction run as the `execution_rw` role.

**REQs.** REQ-EXE-001 (single use by unique constraint on `grant_id` and `nonce`), REQ-EXE-002 (the key is committed before dispatch), REQ-EXE-006 (the sweeper's input), REQ-EXE-008 (the kill switch is read inside the spend), REQ-AUD-001 (Record events appended in the transaction of the object write; an append failure fails the step, EC-E16).

**How the steps map.**
- `Spend` is E8: SERIALIZABLE; checks the grant is `ISSUED`, reads the kill switch, upserts `idempotency_record` (attempt + 1; the same key with other parameters or another action is a collision, EC-E06), inserts `grant_spend` with the key and attempt, marks the grant `SPENT`, appends `GRANT_SPENT` and `DISPATCH_ATTEMPTED`. A grant that is no longer `ISSUED`, or that loses a race, is a replay. A transaction the database refuses with a serialization failure is run again (see "The retry on a serialization failure" below). A collision raises `SECURITY_EVENT IDEMPOTENCY_COLLISION` and returns `controller.ErrKeyCollision`, which the controller answers by voiding the grant.
- `Void` marks the grant `VOID`, inserts one `recovery_request`, enqueues `recovery.requested` and appends `GRANT_VOIDED`, once per grant.
- `Complete` never sees a receipt whose HMAC did not verify: the Controller verifies it (`Controller.completeReceipt`, `KeyRing.VerifyWith` over the canonical JSON of the signed document) before it calls `Complete`, for the receipt of a dispatch and for the sweeper's; a failed verification returns `ErrReceiptSignature`, writes no receipt and appends one `SECURITY_EVENT` `SIGNATURE_INVALID` through `Ledger.Append`, in a transaction of its own (EXE-04, founder's decisions of 28 and 29 Oct; DEC-2026-10-28 §7). If that append fails, nothing is written and the caller gets both errors.
- `Complete` of a `REJECTED_STALE_VERSION` receipt (REQ-EXE-004, EXE-04; PERD v1.18 §38.2) also voids the spent grant in the same transaction, after `RECEIPT`: the grant moves `SPENT → VOID` (migration `0002` lets the trigger allow it only for a confirmed refusal), one `recovery_request` (reason `REJECTED_STALE_VERSION`), the `recovery.requested` event and `GRANT_VOIDED`, whose payload names `from_status` `SPENT`, `to_status` `VOID` and the receipt. `GRANT_SPENT` stays on the Record. If any of these cannot be written, none is, and the sweeper handles the attempt.
- `Complete` is E11: inserts the signed receipt (first writer wins), moves the idempotency record to `RECEIPTED`, enqueues `outcome.verify` and appends `RECEIPT`, in one transaction.
- `Dispatching` lists spent attempts with no receipt.

**Not here.** The `Binder` reads of the intent, the decision and the plan (Trust and the registry are not built), the `Revalidator`, the dispatcher and the sweeper's age threshold. The sweeper writes receipts for every unreceipted attempt it sees; a threshold so that it does not race a dispatch still in flight is configuration (REQ-OPS-003) and is not built.

**Tests.** `go test ./execution/ledger/...` from `services/core/`, against a real PostgreSQL (`internal/testpg`) with the real controller: a clean run in order, 100 concurrent executes of one grant (one dispatch), void and recovery request, kill switch flipped between E6 and E8, crash after the spend and the sweep, a failed Record append failing the spend, a retry under a new grant, key collision, first-writer-wins receipts, grant loading, deadlines.

## StoreBinder (TRU-08, DEBT-23)

`binder.go`: the production `controller.Binder`. Its database half reads, as the tenant's Execution login, the active policy version (`policy_publication`, highest `version_key`) and whether a grant's decision is still an ALLOW under it (`decision_record`). Fails closed: no publication, no decision, a decision that is not an ALLOW, another version and another tenant's grant all answer "no". The intent hash (E3) and the stored plan (E5) come from `IntentSource` and `PlanSource`, which other tasks build and `cmd/core` plugs in; an unwired source is `ErrBinderNotWired`, never an empty answer. The binder reads only the decision's outcome and compares the grant's own policy version with the active one; it does not compare the grant with the decision's `policy_versions_json`. That equality is guaranteed at issue time by the database trigger from #168 (a grant's policy version must equal its decision record's), and the grant is HMAC-bound, so a grant cannot carry another version afterwards. Tests: `go test ./execution/ledger/ -run 'ActivePolicy|StillAllowed|NeverAllowed|AnotherTenants|NeedsADeadline|PassesTheIntent|RecordedDecisionThatIsNot'` (needs PostgreSQL).

## The dispatch budget (RCV-02 slice 2, ruling A153)

The database refuses the third spend of a key: migration `0023` puts a guard on the insert into `grant_spend` that counts the key's `DISPATCH_ATTEMPTED` events in the Record (the key is the grant's tenant, correlation, capability and target; the ceiling is 2, ruling A3 and A62) and raises `NS002`. `Spend` rolls back as a whole, reports `SpendResult.BudgetSpent` and writes nothing; the controller answers `Outcome.BudgetSpent`, the grant stays ISSUED, and the database refuses it again each time. An earlier dispatch event whose key cannot be read counts against every key of its correlation, and a grant whose key cannot be read is refused. Tests: `budget_test.go`.

## The spend lock and the retry on a serialization failure (noema#714, Architect rulings A317 to A328 and A339, relayed, interim)

Spends of one tenant all append to one Record chain head, and PostgreSQL may refuse a SERIALIZABLE spend with SQLSTATE `40001`, at a statement (the idempotency record, the head lock) or at the commit. Such a spend wrote nothing. `Spend` has two layers against it, and one typed answer for when both are used up.

### Layer 1: the tenant's spend lock (A339; `lock.go`, `withSpendLock`)

- **What.** `Spend` takes one connection from the pool, takes an advisory lock for the tenant on it, runs the spend transaction(s) on that same connection, and releases the lock before the connection goes back. The spends of one tenant therefore take turns: each starts its transaction after the previous one committed, so spends of different keys no longer refuse one another with `40001` (they were what three attempts could not cover).
- **Why a session lock before `BEGIN`, not `pg_advisory_xact_lock` as the transaction's first statement.** A SERIALIZABLE transaction takes its snapshot at its first statement, the lock call included. A waiter would be granted the lock after the previous spend committed but would still read the world as it was before, and its first write to the chain head would fail with `40001`. `TestAnAdvisoryXactLockAsTheFirstStatementOfASerializableTransactionDoesNotRefreshItsSnapshot` shows it on the database, and the mutant "transaction-scoped lock as the first statement" fails the eight-key test. This deviates from the wording of A339 ("transaction-scoped", "first statement") for the stated reason; the Architect is asked to confirm it (status file).
- **The key.** `pg_advisory_lock(hashtext(SpendLockNamespace), hashtext(SpendLockName(tenant)))`: the two-key form. In PostgreSQL the two-key form is a key space of its own (`pg_locks.objsubid` 2; the one-key form is 1). Every other advisory lock of the system uses the one-key form (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended(...))` in the Record, the policy publication, the signing keys, the dispatch budget and `recorder`, and `hashtext('noema:migrate')` in the migrator), so none can be this lock. `TestAOneKeyAdvisoryLockWithTheSameNumbersIsAnotherLock` holds the one-key lock of the same two numbers and shows a spend does not wait. The key is per tenant: `TestTheSpendLockIsPerTenant` holds tenant A's lock and spends for tenant B. Two tenants whose names hash alike would only wait for each other.
- **The wait is bounded by the context.** The lock call runs on the caller's context (a deadline is required), so when it ends the waiting call is cancelled and the connection is destroyed: no waiter stays in the lock queue (`TestTheWaitForTheLockEndsWithTheContext`). Chosen over `SET LOCAL lock_timeout`, which would be a second bound next to the context's and a statement of its own, and over a `pg_try_advisory_lock` loop, which polls and queues nobody. A spend that runs out of time waiting returns an error that wraps `context.DeadlineExceeded`, with the grant `ISSUED`; it is the caller's own deadline, not the typed retryable error.
- **No lock stays behind.** The release is ONE deferred step of `withSpendLock` that runs on every way out (commit, replay, refusal, rollback, a serialization failure on every attempt, a context that expired or was already cancelled, a panic, a goroutine that exits), on the same connection that took the lock, with a fresh bounded context (`context.WithoutCancel(ctx)` plus `RollbackTimeout`, never the caller's). A connection that cannot be shown to hold no lock and no transaction (the unlock fails, finds nothing to release, or leaves a transaction open, or the lock call did not complete) is closed, not returned to the pool: closing the session releases its locks. See "Release on every path" for what the answer is when that happens.
- **Cost.** A waiting spend holds one pool connection, so a burst of spends of one tenant can occupy the pool; the holder needs no connection besides its own. The lock covers `Spend` only: other writers of the head (Trust's appends, `Append`, `Complete`) do not take it, which is why layer 2 stays.

### Layer 2: the bounded retry (A317 to A328; `retry.go`, `spendWithRetry`)

- **Attempts.** `Ledger.SerializationAttempts` is the total number of attempts, the first included. It comes from the environment setting `NOEMA_DB_SERIALIZATION_ATTEMPTS` (ruled value 3) and has **no default** (AGENT-16): a `Ledger` with a value below 1 refuses to spend (`ledger: NOEMA_DB_SERIALIZATION_ATTEMPTS is required...`), writing nothing.
- **Where the setting is read.** `Ledger` is not built in `cmd/core` yet; today only tests build it. The composition root that builds it must call `ledger.ParseSerializationAttempts(os.Getenv(ledger.EnvSerializationAttempts))` and refuse to start on its error, which names the setting (missing, empty, zero, negative, signed, padded, non-numeric or out of range). The setting is not yet in `deploy/` (a protected path): see `status/EXE-RETRY-714.md`.
- **Each attempt is a fresh transaction.** Its first statement reads the grant again, so a grant that an earlier winner spent is answered as a replay, and a key whose dispatch budget the winners used up is answered `BudgetSpent`. A failed attempt left nothing behind (idempotency attempt, `grant_spend`, Record events).
- **Only `40001` is retried.** A unique violation, a deadlock (`40P01`) and every other error end the call after one attempt, as before. The context deadline bounds all the attempts: none starts once it is done. There is no pause between attempts.

### The typed retryable error (A339)

When `40001` persists after the last attempt, `Spend` returns `controller.ErrSpendRetryable` wrapped around the database's own error (`errors.Is` matches both; the text is the fixed `ledger: the spend did not commit and the grant is unspent: present it again`, see "Fixed public texts"): nothing was spent, written or dispatched, the grant is still `ISSUED`, and presenting it again can succeed with no change by the caller. This is the answer "try again", as against a refusal. Only `40001` gets it; a unique violation, a deadlock and every other error do not. If the grant has meanwhile become final the answer is a replay, as before. `controller.CodeSpendRetryable` is `DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE`, a `RetryableErrorCode` of `contracts/schemas/errors.v2.json` (checked by a test that reads the contract); no error code was added. `Controller.Execute` already wraps the `Spend` error with `%w`, so the typed error reaches its caller unchanged, with no void, receipt or outcome (`controller/retryable_test.go`; the mutant that wraps with `%v` fails it). The e2e test's `runAll` matches the database's own code through the same chain and is unchanged.

### Fixed public texts (A347, relayed, interim; AGENT-13)

An error that leaves `Spend` has a fixed public text. The tenant's ID and every other identifier (grant, key, nonce), the driver's message (SQLSTATE text, `ERROR:` and `FATAL`, server and login details) and paths are not in `Error()`; the cause is kept behind `Unwrap`, so `errors.Is(err, controller.ErrSpendRetryable)`, `errors.As(err, *pgconn.PgError)`, `errors.Is(err, context.DeadlineExceeded)` and `errors.Is(err, controller.ErrKeyCollision)` still answer. `errors.go` has `fixedError`; `Spend` is `spendOnce` followed by `publicSpendError`, which wraps anything that does not already have a fixed text (the database's error from the insert, the begin and the commit, the Record's error, which names the tenant and the driver) as `ledger: the spend could not be completed`, so a site that was missed cannot leak. Sites with their own text: the grant that does not exist or cannot be read, the nonce that differs, the kill switch, the idempotency record, the grant marked spent, each of the two Record events, the did-not-commit answers, the collision whose event cannot be recorded, the two lock failures (no connection, the wait for the lock). `ParseSerializationAttempts` names the setting and never the value (a value put in the wrong variable may be a secret); the `strconv` error of an out-of-range value stays the cause. `Controller.Execute` wraps with `controller: spend: %w`, which adds no identifier. The rule for tests: they assert on the sentinel and the cause, not on the message (`isDidNotCommit40001` reads the chain).

Not changed here, for the owner of those paths: `Load`, `Void`, `Complete`, `Append`, the binder and `chain.Appender`'s own errors still carry identifiers and driver text; they are not part of this change.

### Release on every path, and a failed release (A348 as refined, relayed by the user; interim, not committed)

`TestTheSpendLockIsReleasedOnEveryPath` reads the server's `pg_locks` (this database only) and the pool's `AcquiredConns` after the call returns: commit; replay; a rolled-back spend (fault that is not 40001); 40001 on every attempt; a context that expires while the transaction runs; a context already cancelled; a panic inside the spend (recovered only in the test); an unlock that fails (EXECUTE on `pg_advisory_unlock` revoked: the connection is destroyed and the lock is gone with its backend); a terminated backend. Also: a spend that waited and then got its turn leaves no waiter, and fifty concurrent spends of five tenants end with no lock and no connection out. A panic inside the spend used to leave the tenant's lock held and one connection out of the pool for good (found by this suite; fixed by the deferred release of `withSpendLock`).

**A failed release never changes the answer of a spend.** If the spend committed and only the unlock fails (refused, nothing to release, or the connection's transaction not idle), `Spend` returns the committed result (`Spent` true, the same result a clean spend gives) and no error, so the controller dispatches; if the spend did not commit, the spend's own error is returned (its fixed public text, its cause); a replay stays a replay; a panic goes on to the caller. In every such case the connection is closed (closing the session ends the lock) and ONE line is logged through the optional `Ledger.Log` (`*slog.Logger`; nil logs nothing): level WARN, message `ledger: the spend lock could not be released cleanly: the connection was closed`, one attribute `event=SPEND_LOCK_RELEASE_FAILED`. No tenant, grant, key, nonce, SQLSTATE or driver text, and no `tenant_id` attribute (PERD 44.2 lists one; this line leaves it out on purpose). The composition root passes a logger that runs through `platform/redaction`. Tests (`unlockfail_test.go`, real database, nothing mocked): the unlock refused by revoking EXECUTE on `pg_advisory_unlock`; an unlock that finds nothing (a trigger runs `pg_advisory_unlock_all()` inside the spend); a panic with and without a failing unlock; the caller's context cancelled by a pgx tracer the moment the commit is acknowledged (the unlock on a fresh context still succeeds and the connection goes back to the pool); a spend that did not commit; a replay; and, end to end through the controller on the real mock-case (`execution/e2e/unlockfail_dispatch_test.go`), the grant is dispatched once, receipted and SPENT with no error.

### Tests

`spendlock_test.go` and `retry_test.go` (need PostgreSQL): `go test ./execution/ledger/ -run 'TestSpendsOfDistinctKeys|TestWithOneAttempt|TestTheSpendLock|TestTheLockIsHeld|TestTheWaitForTheLock|TestAOneKey|TestAPersistent|TestTheControllerReturnsTheTyped|TestAnAdvisory|ThreeAttempts|LosesTheRace|RefusedTwice|LastAttempt|OnlyASerialization|DeadlineStops|WithoutSerializationAttempts|SerializationAttemptsSetting'`.

- Eight grants of eight distinct keys of one tenant, three attempts, every spend made slow so that they overlap: all eight spend, none is refused (three runs).
- The reproduction of noema#714 (a head held and committed by a writer outside the spends, with eight spends behind it, one attempt): exactly one spend is refused with `40001` (the one whose turn it was, on the head), seven spend; before the lock every queued spend was refused.
- Lock: held and released on every way out; per tenant; bounded by the context; the one-key twin is another lock; the transaction-scoped form does not refresh the snapshot.
- Typed error: a persistent `40001` (a trigger on `grant_spend` counts calls in a sequence and raises the SQLSTATE; a real race cannot be made to fail an exact number of times) is `ErrSpendRetryable` after exactly the configured attempts, grant `ISSUED`, nothing on the Record, nothing in `grant_spend`; `23505`, `40P01` and `P0001` are not typed; through a real `Controller`, no dispatch.
- Fixed texts and release: `errtext_test.go` (twenty-one failure classes on the real stack, through the controller, and the setting's parser) and the release suite in `spendlock_test.go`: `go test ./execution/ledger/ -run 'TestEverySpendFailure|TestAKeyCollisionKeeps|TestTheControllersWrapping|TestTheSerializationAttemptsErrors|TestTheSpendLockIsReleased|TestASpendThatWaited|TestFiftyConcurrent'`. Fifty concurrent spends of five tenants: spends of different tenants do not share the lock, and SERIALIZABLE transactions of different tenants still refused one another (33 of 50 committed in one run, the others got the typed retryable error after three attempts); the lock orders the spends of one tenant only.
- Layer 2 as before: eight callers of one grant (one spends, seven replay); a spend refused twice commits on the third attempt; exact attempt counts, non-`40001` codes, the deadline; the strict reading of the setting.
