## Doubts and flags
1. **Which status is the entry's status.** The ruling's entries are (source, source_record_id, facts_digest, status) and the instruction says "the item status the Reconciler gives". The Reconciler gives no status of its own to an item: it maps the item's sealed status (`layer.Item.Status`), and its rows carry that status or UNKNOWN (a KNOWN item whose payload gave a row no field). The facts digest already holds each ROW's status (A365 refined). I used the ITEM's sealed status for the entry (the one T9 reports in `SourceItem.Status`). So a status change moves the digest twice (the row's, when the row has one, and the entry's); the safer reading. If the ruling meant only the row status, the entry status is redundant and a one-line removal. Architect.
2. **The partial index form.** The ruling says "a tuple has at most one decision resting on a confirmation" and "the partial index is fine" without a column list. I chose (tuple, outcome) WHERE confirmation_request_id IS NOT NULL, which is exactly the Writer's `otherState` rule (one decision of an outcome across confirmation states) and so adds no behaviour the Writer lacks. The literal "at most one decision of ANY outcome resting on a confirmation per tuple" (an index over the tuple alone) would also refuse an ALLOW and a REJECT that both rest on confirmations on one tuple; that cannot happen with one request per tuple (a request is accepted or declined, a tuple has one CONFIRM), but the Writer would then need the same cross-outcome rule to return `ErrDecisionConflict` rather than a raw 23505. Not done; Architect's call. The "none versus a request" case of the same outcome stays a Writer-only rule (the NULL state is the older key's).
